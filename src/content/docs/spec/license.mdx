<Aside type="caution" title="This page is an OPEN question">
The license has **not been decided**. This page records the options, the arguments and
the constraints so that the decision is made in public, through the
[RFC process](/governance/rfc-process/), and not by default.

Until it resolves, this specification is published for reading, review and RFC
participation. Implementers SHOULD track this page before shipping a commercial
implementation.
</Aside>

## What is being licensed

Three things, which MAY end up under different terms:

1. **The specification text** — this document set. The strong presumption is that spec
   text MUST be freely readable, quotable and implementable by anyone. No option under
   consideration changes that.
2. **Reference implementations** — libraries and SDKs published by the editor.
3. **The marks** — the names and logos. These are stewarded by the Foundation and are
   **not** covered by whichever software license is chosen. Trademark terms are separate,
   and a conformant implementation MUST NOT imply endorsement by using the marks without
   permission.

## Option A · Apache 2.0

Permissive, from day one, for spec and reference implementations alike.

**For.** Maximum adoption. No adoption friction for enterprises, whose legal review of
Apache 2.0 is a formality. Includes an explicit patent grant, which matters for a
protocol with cryptographic constructions. It is what a serious protocol is expected to
be, and the expectation is itself an asset.

**Against.** Nothing prevents a well-resourced party from taking the reference
implementation, hosting it as a managed service, and out-distributing the originator
before the originator has a business. This is not hypothetical; it is the standard
outcome for infrastructure projects that reach a certain size.

## Option B · BSL 1.1 converting to Apache 2.0

Business Source License on the reference implementations, with an **automatic
conversion** to Apache 2.0 after a fixed change date (commonly 3–4 years per release).
The specification text stays permissive regardless.

**For.** Everything is eventually open, on a published schedule, with no discretion left
to the licensor. Meanwhile a competing managed service of the reference implementation is
restricted during the window where it would be most damaging. Ordinary use — building a
product on it, self-hosting, evaluating, contributing — is unrestricted.

**Against.** BSL is not an OSI-approved open-source license, and some organisations have
blanket policies against non-OSI licenses that no amount of explanation moves. It adds a
compliance question to every adoption conversation. It can read as a signal that the
project is a company first and a protocol second — which is precisely the impression a
Foundation exists to avoid.

## Constraints on any outcome

Whatever is chosen MUST satisfy all of the following. These are not open:

- The **specification text** MUST be freely readable and implementable, with no fee, no
  registration and no permission required.
- An independent implementation MUST be possible without a license from the editor.
- The license MUST NOT discriminate between consumers. A single set of terms applies to
  everyone; there is no privileged licensee.
- Any time-limited restriction MUST have a **published, automatic** expiry. A restriction
  that depends on the licensor's future goodwill MUST NOT be used.
- The chosen license MUST carry an explicit patent grant covering the protocol's
  cryptographic constructions, or be accompanied by a separate irrevocable patent
  non-assertion covenant.
- The marks MUST remain separately stewarded, so that "conformant" stays a statement a
  third party can verify rather than a permission the editor grants.

## How this resolves

1. An RFC is opened proposing one option, with the reasoning stated in full.
2. A public review window of no less than 30 days, per the
   [RFC process](/governance/rfc-process/).
3. Counsel review — the Foundation's jurisdiction is still pending, and the answer may
   depend on where it is formed.
4. Editor merge, a `MINOR` version bump, and a [CHANGELOG](/changelog/) entry recording
   the decision and the rationale.

Until then, the honest statement is the one at the top of this page: it is open, and it
is being decided in the open.

## Interim terms

While this page is marked `open`:

- You MAY read, quote, review, and implement from this specification.
- You MAY build and run implementations, including commercially, at your own risk of a
  later change in the reference-implementation terms. The **specification text** will not
  become more restrictive — that is the one commitment made in advance.
- You MUST NOT use the FlowBond marks to describe your implementation as endorsed,
  certified or official.
- Contributions submitted through the RFC process are accepted on the understanding that
  they will be licensed under whichever terms this page resolves to.
